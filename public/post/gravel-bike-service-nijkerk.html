Servicing or repairing a gravel bike in Nijkerk: what you need to know

A gravel bike sees more dirt, sand and water than a road bike. Read what to look out for when servicing it and where to go in Nijkerk for maintenance and repair.

On a gravel bike you ride asphalt, gravel, sand and forest paths, sometimes all in one ride. That makes it versatile, but also more prone to wear than a bike that mostly rides on dry asphalt. If you look after your gravel bike well, you enjoy it for longer and stand still less often far from home.

Where does a gravel bike wear faster?

Sand and mud work their way into the chain, the cogs and the bearings. Water and dirt also load the brakes. If you ride tubeless, the sealant in the tyres dries up over time and must be replaced. The more varied the surface, the more important it is to check regularly instead of waiting for something to go wrong.

A small routine between services

After a wet or sandy ride, carefully rinse off the worst dirt, without a pressure washer on the bearings, and dry the bike. Clean the chain and lubricate it again. Check the tyre pressure and listen for new noises from the bottom bracket or the head tube. A chain that stays clean and lubricated lasts longer and shifts better.

For those who ride long days off-road

If you ride long tours on varied terrain or start gravel races, you need a bike that does not let you down far from home. You want a workshop that understands what such a day asks of your bike and that takes a strange noise seriously, not only what is on the list.
